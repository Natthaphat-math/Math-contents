Turning Turtles

a game of heads, tails & hidden binary

How it works

A row of coins, one clever trick

You'll face a row of coins, each lying heads-up or tails-up. You and your opponent take turns. Whoever makes the very last move wins.

  1. 1Pick a coin that's heads-up and turn it to tails. Every move needs this part.
  2. 2Optionally, flip one more coin — but only one that sits to the left of the coin you just chose. It can flip either direction.
  3. 3Coins are numbered 1, 2, 3… from the left. That numbering turns out to matter a lot.
  4. 4Once every coin shows tails, nobody can move. Whoever made the last flip wins.

Rules & strategy

How to play

A row of coins is laid out, each showing heads or tails. Players alternate turns.

  1. 1Choose any coin currently showing heads and flip it to tails. This part is required.
  2. 2You may also flip exactly one other coin — but it must sit to the left of the coin from step 1. That one can flip in either direction.
  3. 3If you can't move (no coins are heads-up), you lose. The player who plays the last coin — leaving the whole row tails — wins.

Numbering the coins

Number the coins 1, 2, 3… from the left. This numbering is the whole secret: a coin showing heads at position p behaves exactly like a Nim pile holding p tokens.

The nim-sum

Write every heads-up position in binary and stack them up. XOR them together — add each column of digits and keep only whether the count is odd (1) or even (0), with no carrying. That result is the nim-sum.

Heads-up at positions 5, 6, and 3: 5 = 101 6 = 110 3 = 011 ------- XOR = 000 → nim-sum is 0
  • Nim-sum = 0: whoever moves next is in trouble. Against perfect play, every move they make hands their opponent a winning position.
  • Nim-sum ≠ 0: the player to move has a winning reply — some move brings the nim-sum back to 0.

Finding the winning move

If the nim-sum X isn't 0, look for a heads-up coin at position p where p XOR X is smaller than p. Call that smaller number t.

  • Flip the coin at position p to tails (the required move).
  • If t is 0, stop there — no second flip needed.
  • If t is greater than 0, also flip the coin at position t. It's guaranteed to be to the left of p, so it's always a legal second flip.
Nim-sum X = 6 (110). Heads-up coin at position p = 7 (111). p XOR X = 111 XOR 110 = 001 = 1, and 1 < 7 ✓ → Flip coin 7 to tails, and flip coin 1 too. New nim-sum: 0 — your opponent is now stuck.
