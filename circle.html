Part one

Arcs

Take a circle with center O, and mark two points A and B on it. Those two points cut the circle into two arcs — one short, one long.

The shorter path from A to B is the minor arc, written AB. Two letters are all it needs, since there's only one short way to travel.

The longer path is the major arc. Naming it just "AB" would be ambiguous, so we mark a third point C sitting on that longer path and write ACB — it's still "arc AB", just spelled out unambiguously.

When A and B land at opposite ends of a diameter, the two arcs become equal — each exactly half the circle. Either one is then called a semicircle.

Part two

Central angles

Now draw two radii, OA and OB. The angle they form at the center, AOB, is called a central angle.

A central angle is always measured by the arc it cuts off: arc AB subtends the angle at O, and the two always share the exact same degree measure.
